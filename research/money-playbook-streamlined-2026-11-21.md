# The Money Playbook — The Easy Version

**For:** David Burstein, ~35, Arizona. Recently released after 11.5 years in prison. Broke, no car, living at parents' house. Already has SNAP ($250/mo). Credit was frauded while he was inside — he holds facility letterhead proof. LifeLock is active. He is an authorized user on his parents' high-limit card (believed Amex, seasoned). LLC not formed yet.

**How to use this:** Do it in order. Section 5 is the whole plan as one numbered list — if you read nothing else, read that. Every item has a checkbox. Check it off when it's done.

**The honest headline:** no trick takes a credit score to 850 overnight. Scores run 300–850. The real game is: remove what isn't yours, add good history, keep balances low, and let time work. Done right, a damaged file can gain 50–100 points in 90 days and reach 640–700 in a year. That is the honest math. Everything below is legal only. Anything that needs lying to a lender, a bureau, a school, or a platform is in Section 6 — named as fraud, with the consequence, and excluded.

## 1. FREE MONEY — money you never pay back

### Business credit (the slow ladder — starts now, pays off in a year)
- **What it is:** Credit in the business name, built step by step. LLC → EIN → D-U-N-S number → net-30 vendor accounts → store cards → business cards.
- **What you get:** 90 days: a few hundred in vendor terms. 12 months: **$1,000–$5,000** in business credit lines.
- **What it costs:** $50 for the LLC. EIN is free. D-U-N-S is free (about 30 business days — do not pay for it).
- **How long:** 12 months to real lines. Never produces cash in the first 90 days.
- **First step:** Form the LLC, get the EIN, request the D-U-N-S number, open the business bank account. Pay every vendor bill 15–20 days early — early payment is what builds the score.
- [ ] LLC + EIN + D-U-N-S requested
- [ ] 2–5 net-30 vendor accounts, paid early

### What a loan can never do
There is no legal way to borrow $1M from zero income and zero capital — not with a trust, not with an EIN, not with a life insurance policy. That was tested three times with real sources. The closest real things are the ladder above: income → small policy → business credit → microloan. Anyone selling a $1M-from-nothing trick is selling the course, not the trick.

## 3. CREDIT CARDS — spending power, not cash

### Secured card (your own card — do this first)
- **What it is:** You put down a deposit. That deposit IS your credit limit. It reports to all three bureaus every month.
- **What you get:** **Discover it Secured — $200 deposit = $200 limit.** No annual fee. Graduates to a regular card in about 7–8 months and the deposit comes back.
- **What it costs:** $200, refundable.
- **How long:** Card in hand in about 7–10 days.
- **First step:** Apply for the Discover it Secured. Use it for one small thing a month. Pay it off. The trick: pay BEFORE the statement closing date (not the due date) so the bureau sees a $0–$10 balance, not a $200 balance. On a $200 card, even a $60 balance is 30% used.
- [ ] Discover it Secured applied for
- [ ] First month paid, before the statement closes

### Backup card (if Discover says no)
- **What it is:** OpenSky Secured Visa — no credit check required.
- **What you get:** Deposit = limit ($300–$3,000).
- **What it costs:** Deposit + a small annual fee on some versions. Check the terms before applying.
- **First step:** Only if Discover declines you.
- [ ] Only if needed

### Unsecured card (month 2–3)
- **What it is:** A real card with no deposit, for thin files.
- **What you get:** **Chase Freedom Rise — $500–$1,500 limit.** $0 annual fee.
- **What it costs:** $0 to apply. Interest 18–28% if you carry a balance — don't carry one.
- **How long:** Apply once, at month 2–3, after the secured card is reporting.
- **First step:** Wait. Do not apply now. One application at a time, weeks apart. Five applications in a week looks desperate and gets denials.
- [ ] Apply at month 2–3

### 0% cards (a year from now, not now)
- **What it is:** Cards with 0% interest for 12–21 months.
- **What you get:** Free borrowing for a year — but only once your score is about 670+.
- **What it costs:** $0 if paid off before the intro ends. Watch out: some are "deferred interest" — if you miss the payoff date, all the interest comes back at once.
- **First step:** None now. This is the 12-month goal.
- [ ] 12-month goal — revisit when the score is 670+

### Cash advances (the expensive emergency only)
- **What it is:** Cash from a credit card.
- **What you get:** Cash fast.
- **What it costs:** 5% fee up front + about 30% interest, no grace period. $1,000 costs about $75 in 30 days, about $127 in 90 days.
- **First step:** Only for $500 or less, paid back in 2–3 weeks. Never for $1,000+ carried for months. And you can't take a $1,000 advance until you HAVE a $3,000+ limit — that's 12+ months out anyway.
- [ ] Know the cost before ever using one

## 4. FIX CREDIT + BOOST SCORE — the deep dive

Your score is dragged down by two things: accounts that aren't yours (fraud while you were inside) and a thin file (almost no history of your own). Fix those two things and the score moves. Here is every tool, in order of power.

### The #1 move: block the fraud (FCRA 605B)
- **What it is:** A federal law that forces a credit bureau to block — hide — information that came from identity theft. This is stronger than a regular dispute. It is the single biggest lever you have.
- **What you get:** Every fraudulent account, inquiry, and collection removed from your file. This is likely worth **50–100+ points** once it lands, because fraud accounts are the heaviest weight on your file.
- **What it costs:** $0.
- **How long:** The bureau must block it within **4 business days** of getting the complete paperwork. The full clean-up takes about 30 days.
- **First step — the exact steps:**
  1. Pull all three reports free at AnnualCreditReport.com (free weekly reports, permanent now). Mark every account you don't recognize.
  2. File an identity theft report at IdentityTheft.gov (free). It makes your FTC Identity Theft Report and a recovery plan.
  3. Send a separate 605B block letter to EACH bureau that shows fraud — one bureau's block does not reach the other two. Include: your ID, proof of address, the FTC Identity Theft Report, the list of fraudulent items, and your facility letterhead proof (it proves you were incarcerated when the accounts were opened — that is unusually strong evidence).
  4. Send it certified mail, return receipt. Keep copies of everything.
  5. Experian disputes by mail go to: Experian, P.O. Box 4500, Allen, TX 75013. Check the other two bureaus' current addresses on their sites before mailing.
- **Why yours is strong:** Most people can't prove where they were when the fraud happened. You can — the facility letterhead shows you were locked up. A card opened in your name while you were inside is provably not yours.
- [ ] All three reports pulled
- [ ] FTC Identity Theft Report filed at IdentityTheft.gov
- [ ] 605B block letters sent to all three bureaus (certified mail)

### Step 2: freeze + fraud alert (lock the door)
- **What it is:** A fraud alert tells lenders to check extra hard before opening anything in your name. A freeze locks your file so nothing new can open at all.
- **What you get:** No new fraud accounts. A freeze is stronger than an alert.
- **What it costs:** $0. Both are free by federal law.
- **How long:** Minutes online. A fraud alert lasts 1 year (7 years with an identity theft report). A freeze lasts until you lift it.
- **First step:** Place a fraud alert at one bureau — it must tell the other two. Then place a freeze at all three, one by one. You can lift a freeze temporarily any time you need to apply for something.
- [ ] Fraud alert placed
- [ ] Freeze placed at all three bureaus

### Step 3: dispute anything else that is wrong (FCRA disputes)
- **What it is:** Your legal right to challenge anything inaccurate on your reports — wrong balance, wrong date, account that isn't yours, duplicate collection.
- **What you get:** Anything the bureau can't verify in 30 days must come off.
- **What it costs:** $0.
- **How long:** The bureau has **30 days** to investigate (45 if you send more info during the 30 days). It must tell the furnisher within 5 business days and send you the written result within 5 business days after it finishes.
- **First step:** For each wrong item, write one short letter per bureau: your name, address, date of birth, the account name and number, one sentence on what is wrong, what it should say instead, and copies (never originals) of your proof. Certified mail. Do NOT dispute accurate information — it doesn't work and it can backfire by "verifying" the bad item fresh.
- **Key rule:** Dispute through the BUREAU, not just the lender. That is what puts the furnisher's legal duty to investigate into play.
- [ ] Wrong items listed
- [ ] Dispute letters sent

### Step 4: your parents' card (already working for you — protect it)
- **What it is:** You are an authorized user on your parents' high-limit card. Its whole history — age, limit, payment record — shows on your file.
- **What you get:** This is already lifting your score. A Federal Reserve study found 46.8% of thin-file subprime borrowers moved up a tier from an authorized-user account. It is worth roughly 3–6 months of time on the card lane.
- **What it costs:** $0.
- **How long:** Already working.
- **Watch out — it cuts both ways:** if your parents carry a high balance on that card, the high utilization hits YOUR file too. If they miss a payment, it hits your file at Equifax and TransUnion (Experian ignores late payments on authorized-user accounts, the other two don't). Check the card's balance and payment history. If the balance is high, ask them to pay it down — or ask to be removed and re-added later. And know this: mortgage underwriters often ignore authorized-user accounts in manual review. The card lifts your score, but it is not the same as your own history.
- [ ] Card balance + payment history checked

### Step 5: the utilization trick (free points, every month)
- **What it is:** Your score cares most about how much of your limit you use — and it only sees the balance on the statement closing date.
- **What you get:** Dropping utilization from high to under 10% can add **20–40 points** in one cycle.
- **What it costs:** $0.
- **How long:** One billing cycle.
- **First step:** Pay your card BEFORE the statement closing date, not the due date. On a $200 secured card, keep the reported balance under $20 — ideally $0–$10. Pay twice a month if you use the card a lot. Never let a card report more than 30% used.
- [ ] First payment made before the statement closes

### Step 6: Experian Boost (free, 5 minutes)
- **What it is:** Experian lets you add on-time phone, utility, internet, streaming, and some rent payments to your Experian file.
- **What you get:** Average **+13 FICO points** instantly. Only positive payments count — a late bill never hurts you here.
- **What it costs:** $0.
- **How long:** About 5 minutes to connect your bank account.
- **First step:** Sign up free at Experian, connect the bank account you pay bills from, verify the payments it finds.
- **Limit:** It only touches your Experian file. Lenders who pull Equifax or TransUnion won't see it. And some mortgage lenders use older FICO versions that ignore Boost. Still free, still worth it.
- [ ] Experian Boost turned on

### Step 7: credit-builder loan (adds the missing piece — an installment loan)
- **What it is:** A small loan where the money sits in a locked savings account while you pay it off. You get the money back at the end, minus interest. Every payment reports to all three bureaus.
- **What you get:** A second type of account on your file (installment loan + credit card = better "credit mix") and 24 months of on-time payments. Worth about **10–20 points** over the first few months, more over time.
- **What it costs:** **Self, $25 a month plan:** you pay $600 over 24 months, get back about $520. Total cost about **$80**. The $35 plan: pay $840, get back about $720, cost about $120. APR 15.5–15.9%.
- **How long:** 24 months. Reporting starts after the first payment.
- **First step:** Open the Self $25 plan. Set it to autopay from your bank account so a payment is never late. One late payment on this hurts more than the loan helps.
- **Skip this one:** MoneyLion Credit Builder Plus — $19.99 a month in membership fees alone ($240 a year) before interest, plus a CFPB settlement over its membership practices. Self is cheaper and cleaner.
- [ ] Self $25 plan opened + autopay on

### Step 8: rent reporting (only if you pay rent)
- **What it is:** A service that reports your on-time rent payments to the bureaus as a tradeline.
- **What you get:** One more positive tradeline on all three bureaus.
- **What it costs:** Self rent-only reporting is **free**. Boom is $5 a month ($60 a year) and needs no landlord. RentReporters is $94.95 setup + $10.95 a month — skip it, too expensive.
- **How long:** Reports start in 10–30 days.
- **First step:** Honest check — you live at your parents' house. If you don't pay formal rent under a lease, this lane is CLOSED for now. Don't pay for a service that has nothing to report. Revisit it when you rent your own place.
- [ ] Rent situation checked — open or closed

### Step 9: goodwill letter (free, low odds, worth one stamp)
- **What it is:** A short, polite letter to a creditor asking them to remove a late payment as a courtesy.
- **What you get:** Sometimes a late mark disappears. Success is under 10% — but it costs a stamp.
- **What it costs:** $0.
- **How long:** Weeks to hear back. Often no reply at all.
- **First step:** Only for a REAL late payment on an account that is now current with a long clean stretch after it. One page: what happened, that it was isolated, that you have paid on time since, and a direct ask to remove it. Only works with the original creditor — a collector can't remove the original creditor's mark.
- [ ] Goodwill letter sent (only for a real, isolated late)

### Step 10: pay-for-delete (a negotiation, not a right)
- **What it is:** You offer to pay a collection account in exchange for the collector deleting it from your reports.
- **What you get:** A collection gone entirely — if they agree.
- **What it costs:** Whatever you negotiate. Often a settlement for less than the full amount.
- **How long:** Weeks.
- **First step:** Dispute inaccuracies FIRST (free removals). Only then, for a real debt, offer pay-for-delete in writing. Get the agreement IN WRITING before you pay a dollar. Big banks almost never agree. Small collectors sometimes do, especially on old, small debts. No law forces them to agree — the FCRA actually requires accurate reporting, so bureaus reject deletions of accurate, verifiable items.
- [ ] Inaccuracies disputed first
- [ ] Pay-for-delete offered in writing (only for real debts)

### Step 11: rapid rescore (only when a lender needs it)
- **What it is:** A lender-only fast-track that updates your file in days instead of a billing cycle.
- **What you get:** An updated score in **3–5 business days** after a real change (a balance paid down, an error fixed).
- **What it costs:** $25–$40 per account per bureau. The LENDER pays it. The FCRA forbids charging you for correcting information.
- **How long:** 3–5 business days.
- **First step:** None now. This is a tool for later — when you apply for the microloan or a mortgage and need a fresh score fast. You cannot order it yourself. Any company selling you a "rapid rescore" directly is lying.
- [ ] Later — only through a lender

### Step 12: ask for limit increases (month 6+)
- **What it is:** Once a card has 6 months of perfect payments, ask the issuer for a higher limit.
- **What you get:** A higher limit lowers your utilization without you spending less. Worth about **10–20 points**.
- **What it costs:** $0 — but ask first whether it is a soft pull or a hard pull. A hard pull costs a few points.
- **First step:** At month 6, ask Discover for an increase on the graduated card.
- [ ] Month 6 — ask Discover

No trick takes anyone to 850 overnight. The scale is 300–850. Here is what the moves above realistically do for a thin, damaged file like yours, if you do them all:

## 6. WHAT NOT TO DO — scams and illegal tricks, one line each

- **CPNs ("credit privacy numbers")** — a fake 9-digit number sold as a new identity. Illegal. Federal crime (identity fraud / synthetic identity). Often a stolen Social Security number belonging to a child, a senior, or someone in prison. The FTC, CFPB, and FBI all warn against it. Prison time.
- **Fake pay stubs, fake employer letters, fake tax returns, fake 1099s** — bank fraud, 18 U.S.C. § 1014 (up to $1M fine / 30 years). Lying on the FAFSA is federal student-aid fraud, 20 U.S.C. § 1097. You just got out after 11.5 years — a fraud conviction ends everything you are building.
- **Buying authorized-user tradelines from strangers** — the issuer's rules forbid selling AU access and the account can be closed; FICO's anti-abuse filters detect purchased tradelines and discount them; the stranger can remove you any time and you have no recourse. Your parents' card is the real one — free, genuine, and it passes the filter.
- **Disputing accurate information** — doesn't work, and it can backfire by re-verifying the bad item.
- **Credit-repair companies that charge upfront** — illegal under the Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA). They can't take a dollar until the work is done. Everything they sell, you can do yourself for free.
- **Closing old accounts** — kills your average account age and your available credit. Leave them open.
- **Applying for five cards at once** — each application is a hard inquiry that costs a few points and stays on your report 2 years. One at a time, weeks apart.
- **"Remove accurate negatives" promises** — nobody can legally remove accurate negative information. Anyone who says they can is selling a scam.
- **Paying anyone to "release" unclaimed money** — the search and the claim are free. A fee demand is a scam.
- **Borrowing from a "trust / EIN / life-insurance" scheme** — tested three times with real sources. There is no $1M-from-nothing. The only people paid are the ones selling the course.

## APP SPEC — what automates (the credit-repair app)

**The idea:** an app that automates Section 4 of this playbook. The owner is the first user — he is rebuilding his own credit after 11.5 years inside, so he dogfoods it. What follows is what automates, what needs the user, and where the legal lines are.

### 1. Pulling the reports
There is no free consumer API for bureau reports. Three paths, in order:
- **V1 — user uploads the PDFs.** The user pulls free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com and uploads them. Free, no credentialing, no permission needed. The app parses the PDFs into tradelines, balances, dates, and inquiries from there. This is the launch path.
- **V2 — licensed reseller API.** Companies like pull.credit, CRS Credit API, and Soft Pull Solutions sell tri-merge reports (all three bureaus, soft pull by default) through a REST API. But the app's business needs a FCRA permissible purpose, credentialing/onboarding, a consent flow, an audit trail, and it pays a fee per pull. Not a day-one feature.
- **What needs the user:** identity verification, consent, and the AnnualCreditReport.com pull itself (until V2 is credentialed).

### 2. Error detection — what the app flags automatically
From the parsed reports, the app can flag without a human:
- Accounts opened while he was incarcerated (open date inside the prison window) — his strongest auto-flag.
- Accounts that don't match his identity (name, address, or SSN fragments that aren't his) — mixed files.
- Duplicate accounts (same creditor, same balance, different account numbers).
- Balances that don't match statements the user uploads.
- Negative items older than 7 years from the date of first delinquency (past the reporting clock).
- Late payments on accounts that show paid-on-time everywhere else.
- Inquiries he doesn't recognize.
- **What needs the user:** one tap per flagged item — "is this yours, yes or no?" — and uploading proof (statements, the facility letter).

### 3. Dispute-letter generation + the 30-day clock
- **Templated per dispute type:** not-mine / identity theft (605B), wrong balance, wrong date, duplicate, outdated (past 7 years), mixed file. The app fills in account numbers, dates, and the user's facts.
- **One letter per bureau, per item.** A fix at one bureau does not reach the other two.
- **Clock tracking:** the 30-day investigation clock starts when the bureau receives the dispute. The app logs the receipt date (certified mail return receipt, or the bureau portal confirmation), counts down, and reminds at day 25 to follow up. It logs results and re-disputes with new evidence if an item is verified wrongly.
- **What must stay human:** the user reviews and approves every letter before it goes — his tap. Mailing is certified mail (strongest paper trail) or the bureau's online portal (faster, weaker record) — offer both.
- **One trap:** furnishers may skip a dispute they reasonably believe a credit repair company prepared (Regulation V, 12 C.F.R. § 1022.43(b)(2)). The letters must read as the user's own words about his own facts — not a form the app blasted out.

### 4. Utilization coaching
- The app watches card balances and statement closing dates (bank link, or the user enters them by hand). It tells him the exact move: "Your Discover statement closes in 3 days. Balance is $87. Pay $70 now so it reports $17."
- It tracks utilization month to month and shows the points impact.
- **What needs the user:** connecting the accounts or entering balances, and the payment itself — his tap.

### 5. The legal engine vs. the grey area — named honestly
- **The real engine (legal, and it works):** FCRA disputes of INACCURATE items only, 605B identity-theft blocks, goodwill letters. These are the user's actual rights. Real fixes stick.
- **The grey-area tactic:** "dispute everything and hope the furnisher misses the 30-day window." Honest label: it sometimes works — an item the furnisher doesn't verify in time must be deleted. But: the bureau can label repeat disputes frivolous and stop investigating (it must notify within 5 business days); a deleted item can be RE-INSERTED if the furnisher later certifies it as accurate (the bureau must notify the user); and this tactic is exactly what the FTC and CFPB target in credit-repair enforcement. Fake wins come back.
- **The decision for this app:** it disputes only items the user flags as wrong or not-his, with the reason stated. It never files a dispute the user knows is false. That is the legal line and the better business.

### 6. CROA — how the app can charge
The Credit Repair Organizations Act covers any business selling credit repair. The rules:
- **No upfront fees.** The company cannot take a dollar until the promised service is done.
- **Written contract** with the services, total cost, guarantees, and timeline — plus the "Consumer Credit File Rights" disclosure before signing.
- **3-day cooling-off period** to cancel free.
- **Telemarketing Sales Rule** (if selling by phone): no charge until 6+ months after results are shown on a report.
- **Practical model:** free to use the tools (upload, error scan, letter drafts). Charge a monthly subscription only AFTER a service is delivered — e.g., after the first dispute is filed and the clock is running, or per confirmed deletion. Never charge before the work is done. And the app must tell users they can dispute for free themselves — CROA requires the disclosure, and it is true.

### 7. What must stay human
The user's tap on every letter before it goes. The user's yes/no on "is this account mine?" Uploading proof documents. Any phone call to a creditor or collector. The decision to pay a debt (pay-for-delete, settlement). The FAFSA, loan, and GoFundMe steps — outside the app.
