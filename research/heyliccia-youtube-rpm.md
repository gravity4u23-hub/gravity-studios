## Transcript (cleaned, full content)

RPM is the single most important factor in how much money a YouTube channel makes — more important than view count. A super low RPM and a bad audience means even tens or hundreds of millions of views won't pay enough.

One faceless YouTube channel made him over $300,000 (he refreshes the analytics on camera to prove it; currency USD). Another channel using the same tactics had $500 days and made $11,000 in its lifetime.

RPM = how much YouTube pays per 1,000 views.

Example 1: a police body-cam video, 1.8M views, made $7,800, RPM $4.26. It should have made $10–15k (good niche, higher RPM expected) but the video wasn't monetized until September 1st — it was the channel's first video, so the first ~500,000 views earned nothing while he waited on 1,000 subscribers + watch hours + monetization approval. Missing $3–5k.

Example 2: a kids/family video, 1.7M views (100k FEWER views), made $13,500, RPM ~$8 — nearly double. Why: it was 33 minutes long vs 17 minutes. Average view duration was longer in absolute minutes even though the percentage viewed was lower (25.7% vs 46%). Longer videos = more ad inventory = more money. "Longer videos equal more money and it's that simple." Don't sacrifice quality just to stretch a video — a solid 10-minute video should stay 10 minutes — but to make $5–10k+/month with faceless channels, longer videos matter because people watch longer videos anyway.

Audience matters: "who's watching equals how much you get paid." Viewers with money = higher RPM, because advertisers pay more when the audience can actually buy. Adults with jobs = higher chance of spending. High kid audience = lower RPM (kids don't have their own money). Same with countries where people make less money (India, Brazil, Philippines): more views, but RPM averages down. Shorts channels with hundreds of millions of views often have audiences that are ~50% Brazil, ~30% India — "our RPM is gonna be complete shit."

The kids video still got $8 RPM because most viewers were actually 25–34 and 34–44 (70–80% of the audience) — kids watching on parents' phones/tablets/TVs/accounts. The body-cam channel's audience is mostly 25+, ~13% are 65+, ~21% are 25–34, ~22% are 44+ — people with bank accounts, savings, jobs.

Conclusion: get into a niche where the main audience is in the USA and older, make videos 20–30 minutes long, and it pays extremely well. Secret: with higher RPM you don't need as many views — to make $5,000/month you normally need ~1M views, but in a high-RPM niche with a good US audience you need only ~300–400k views.
