# PART A — TRIPLE CHECK of the loan/trust hunt

The earlier hunt (~/workspace/research/loan-trust-loophole-hunt-2026-11-21.md) tested the viral sirenpatriot video — "$1M whole-life policy (~$100/mo), EIN, trust, borrow $1M against it" — and found no loophole from zero income/capital. This part re-tests each key claim with **independent sources** (different from the ones the hunt used).

## A1. No lender lends against face value / death benefit while the insured is alive and healthy — HOLDS

**Hunt's source:** Kensington Financial FAQ — "we only lend against the guaranteed cash value, up to 95%"; "Will you loan against my death benefit? No."

**Independent source 1 — Colonial Penn (an insurer, colonialpenn.com):** "While you can't take out a loan on your policy's death benefit, you can borrow against your policy's cash balance." Same rule from the other side of the market: the insurer itself says the death benefit is not borrowable.

**Independent source 2 — Insure.com:** on the death benefit — "Can you borrow against it? No." (Found in triple-check searches.)

**Independent source 3 — Infinite Wealth Group:** "excess death benefit above the loan balance counts for $0 of collateral while the insured is alive." Even the *excess* — the part of the face value above any loan — is worth zero as collateral during life.

**Independent source 4 — Wall Street Journal:** policy loans let you "borrow up to 90% or more of your cash surrender value." The ceiling is always expressed as a percentage of cash surrender value, never of face value.

**Verdict: HOLDS, four times over.** Insurer, lender, consumer guide, and financial press all agree: the borrowing base is cash surrender value only.

## A2. Premium finance floors ($5–10M net worth, $150k–500k income, $2–2.5M face) — HOLDS. Kai-Zen lowest bar — CORRECTED UPWARD

**Independent source 1 — Enterprise Bank & Trust's own premium-finance page:** $10M net worth + $500,000/yr premium. Confirms the top end of the hunt's range from a lender's own page.

**Independent source 2 — Penny Pincher premium-finance guide:** $5M minimum net worth, $10–30M+ for large cases, income $500k+. Confirms the range from a second independent source.

**Kai-Zen correction — NIW's own site (group.simplicityniw.com / kaizenpolicy.com):**
- "Employer offering employee benefits: Employee income $100,000… Individuals — Household income $200,000. Ages 18–65."
- A second NIW FAQ page repeats it: "Client Income: Greater than $100,000 per year if corporately sponsored, $200,000 if individually funded."
- Minimum contribution: **$21,350/yr** ($20,000 + $1,350 trust fee), 5 years.
- One NIW/Allianz cover letter (2019) required **$1M net worth** for earners in the $100k–199k band.
- Another Kai-Zen guide puts the suitability bar at $200,000 household income **and** $500,000+ net worth.

**Verdict: the traditional premium-finance floors HOLD. The Kai-Zen "lowest bar" from the hunt ($100k income + ~$22k/yr) was WRONG for an individual — the real floor is $200,000 household income + $21,350/yr for 5 years (+ net worth requirements that vary by carrier).** The hunt's conclusion is strengthened, not weakened: the lowest door in the entire premium-finance universe is twice as far away as first reported. Even at $200k income, Kai-Zen hands you no cash now — it builds a retirement policy over 12–18 years.

## A3. SBA: life insurance required only when the loan is not fully secured AND the business depends on one owner; amount = collateral shortfall; cheap $1M term (~$76–95/mo) can satisfy it — HOLDS

**Source — SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), Section A, Chapter 5, Paragraph C,** confirmed via multiple secondary sources (quote.planconnections.com, pienzawealthpartners.org, 4businessinsurance.com):
- Life insurance is **required** only when (a) the loan is NOT fully secured by discounted collateral, AND (b) the business depends on one owner (sole proprietorship, single-member LLC, or one-owner-dependent business).
- The required amount = **the collateral shortfall** — the gap between the loan and the discounted collateral value. It does NOT increase the loan amount.
- The lender takes a collateral assignment; the assignee's claim is limited to the unpaid loan balance.
- Term insurance is enough — the SBA does not require whole life. The borrower pays the premiums.

**Verdict: HOLDS.** The policy is a key that can let a borderline SBA loan through the paperwork — it never adds a dollar to the loan amount. The money comes from the business's merits and the owner's credit.

## A4. Strawman / EIN / trust-fund theories are frivolous (IRS Notice 2010-33, $5,000 penalty per filing) — HOLDS

**Hunt's sources:** IRS Notice 2010-33; 26 U.S.C. § 6702 ($5,000 per frivolous return/submission); § 6673 (up to $25,000 Tax Court penalty); US v. Benabe; FBI 2011 bulletin; originator Roger Elvick convicted.

**Independent source 1 — IRS Revenue Ruling 2005-21:** the strawman/redemption position "has no merit and is frivolous." A second, separate IRS authority reaching the same conclusion.

**Independent source 2 — IRS, "The Truth About Frivolous Tax Arguments":** the notion of secret Treasury accounts assigned to each citizen "is pure fantasy."

**Verdict: HOLDS.** Two separate IRS authorities plus the courts plus the FBI. There is no statute, regulation, lender, or mechanism that turns an EIN or a trust into money. The only people paid in this scheme are the ones selling the trust package or course.

## A5. The PV arithmetic — RECOMPUTED with my own assumptions, same conclusion

The hunt computed: $1M death benefit on a healthy 35-year-old worth ~$111k today vs ~$214k PV of premiums = net ~−$103k. I recomputed with **different assumptions** to test whether the conclusion depends on the hunt's numbers:

**My assumptions (stated plainly):** healthy 35-year-old man, lives to 82 (47 more years), discount rate 4%, premium $1,000/month ($12,000/year — the middle of the hunt's live-quote range).
- PV of the $1M death benefit: $1,000,000 ÷ 1.04^47 = $1,000,000 ÷ 6.317 ≈ **$158,300**
- PV of the premiums: $12,000/yr × annuity factor [(1 − 1.04^−47) ÷ 0.04 = 21.04] ≈ **$252,500**
- Net value to any buyer: $158,300 − $252,500 ≈ **−$94,200** (before the buyer's profit margin)

**Verdict: HOLDS under independent assumptions.** Even with a longer life (which raises the benefit's present value) and a lower discount rate (which also raises it), the policy is still worth roughly **negative $94,000–$103,000** to any third party. That is why no settlement buyer, lender, or premium-finance desk will pay anything for a new policy on a young healthy person. It is not a rule or a conspiracy; it is the time value of money minus the cost of keeping the policy alive. The number only turns positive if the insured is old or sick (short horizon) or if real cash has already been paid in (cash value).

## A6. Did the hunt MISS anything? Three doors tested

### (a) Any lender that accepts face value as collateral for a LIVING borrower, in any structure?
**Tested and closed.** Insurer policy loans (75–90% of cash value), third-party policy lenders (Kensington: 95% of guaranteed cash value, "No" to death benefit), insurance-backed lines of credit (The Bancorp IBLOC: up to 95% of cash value, $75k minimum, policy in force 12+ months), collateral assignment (loan amount set by normal underwriting of the borrower, not the face value), premium finance (floors above), life settlements (age 65+ or terminal illness only). No structure — trust, LLC, ILIT, split-dollar — changes the borrowing base. The trust changes WHO borrows, not HOW MUCH.

### (b) Any state or federal program that lends against life insurance?
**Tested — one real program exists, and it still doesn't help.** The VA / U.S. Government Life Insurance program lets veterans borrow against their government life insurance policies — up to **94% of the cash value or reserve** (38 CFR 6.13, 8.13). Two bars: (1) it is **veterans only** — David is not a veteran; (2) it is still **cash-value based, not face-value based**. No state or federal program lends against a policy's face value. This door is closed twice over for him.

### (c) Can a trust pledge a policy to a private / hard-money lender on different terms than a bank?
**Tested and closed.** Hard-money and private lenders lend against **real estate** at 65–75% LTV, or against a business's receivables. A policy with ~$0 cash value is not collateral they accept at any LTV. A trust can be the borrower (and specialty trust lenders exist for trust-owned real estate), but the borrowing base is still the collateral's real value. A trust-owned policy with no cash value pledges nothing.

### A6 verdict: NOTHING MISSED. No new door opens.

## B1. Business credit via EIN — the real ladder

**Exact steps:**
1. **Form an LLC in Arizona** — Arizona Corporation Commission, online. Cost: $50 filing fee (standard; verify at azcc.gov at filing time). Get an operating agreement (free templates exist; keep it simple).
2. **EIN from the IRS** — free, issued immediately online at irs.gov. Anyone charging for one is charging for a form.
3. **Business bank account** in the entity's exact legal name. Never mix personal and business funds. (He has 2 bank accounts already — a dedicated business account is a separate step.)
4. **D-U-N-S Number from Dun & Bradstreet** — free; standard processing typically ~30 business days. D&B's paid tier buys ~8-business-day speed, not legitimacy. Do not pay for DUNS.
5. **Net-30 vendor accounts (months 0–3):** suppliers who invoice and give 30 days to pay — office supplies, packaging, shop materials, things he already buys. Only vendors that **report** to D&B / Experian Business / Equifax Business build the file — most vendors do NOT report, and reporting is voluntary. Ask before signing up. Known reporters include Uline, Grainger, Quill, Strategic Network Solutions (verify current reporting before relying on any one name). **Pay early — 15–20 days before due.** D&B's PAYDEX score runs 1–100; 80 = paying on time, 100 = paying 30 days early. Early payment is what moves the score.
6. **Store / fleet cards (months 3–6):** accounts tied to the EIN; many report to business bureaus even when they start with a personal guarantee.
7. **Business credit cards (months 6–12):** revolving credit in the business name. Read whether the issuer reports to business bureaus, personal bureaus, or both — it varies.
8. **Bank / institutional credit (months 12+):** lines of credit, equipment finance, term debt underwritten on the business.

Do not pay for that promise.

**What business credit does NOT do:** it never produces a cash loan in the first 90 days, and it never produces $1M. It is a slow ladder to hundreds, then low thousands.

## B2. SBA microloans (up to $50k) and 7(a) (up to $5M)

**SBA Microloan — the most realistic SBA door for him:**
- **Max $50,000; average ~$13,000** (SBA.gov via multiple sources). Rates generally **8–13%**, set by the intermediary; max term 7 years.
- Made by **nonprofit intermediary lenders** (often CDFIs), not by the SBA directly. The intermediary makes the credit decision and sets the terms — requirements vary by intermediary.
- **No SBA-wide minimum time in business and no revenue requirement.** Some intermediaries fund true pre-revenue startups; others want up to 6 months of sales. A written business plan + projections + owner experience carry the weight when there is no revenue.
- Most intermediaries prefer personal credit **620+** (some accept 575+), look at cash-flow projections, and typically require **collateral plus a personal guarantee**.
- Cannot be used to pay existing debt or buy real estate. Eligible: working capital, inventory, supplies, furniture, fixtures, machinery, equipment.
- **Funding speed: 30–60+ days.** Many intermediaries require business training/technical assistance alongside the loan.
- **His realistic shot:** pre-revenue but with a real business plan (GEO audits, concierge, dead-lead reactivation, Missed-Call Rescue are real, named businesses with real offers), the fraud-block letters in progress, and the AU tradeline — a $10,000–$15,000 microloan is plausible at a mission-driven Arizona intermediary (search the SBA microloan intermediary directory at sba.gov for Arizona CDFIs) once his personal score is back above ~600 and he has a plan + projections. At $0 income and a damaged score today, it is a 60–90-day project, not a this-week loan.
- **What changes the answer:** 6+ months of business bank deposits (revenue-based underwriting opens up); a personal score above 620; collateral (equipment/inventory being purchased counts); completion of the intermediary's training.

**SBA 7(a) — up to $5M:**
- The SBA does not set a single time-in-business minimum, but individual lenders typically want **2+ years in business, revenue, and collateral**. A brand-new business with no revenue is not a 7(a) borrower.
- **SBA Community Advantage (up to $350,000)** through participating lenders is the middle rung — still wants a track record, but mission-focused lenders are more flexible than banks.
- **What changes the answer:** 2 years of business tax returns showing revenue, a DSCR (debt-service coverage ratio) of ~1.15–1.25x, collateral covering most of the loan, and a personal score of 680+. That is a 2-year project from where he stands.
- **The life-insurance key (Part A3):** once he IS a 7(a) applicant, a cheap $1M term policy (~$76–95/mo) satisfies the insurance requirement when there is a collateral shortfall. It unlocks paperwork, not money.

## B4. Leveraging the AU tradeline — what it actually does

**What a seasoned high-limit AU tradeline does:**
- It imports the account's **age, limit, payment history, and utilization** onto his report. A years-old, high-limit, perfectly-paid Amex is the best possible AU account: it lengthens average age of accounts (15% of FICO), adds on-time history (35%), and lowers overall utilization (30%) — the three biggest scoring factors at once.
- A Federal Reserve study (FEDS 2010-23) simulated adding one old, perfectly-paid $15,000-limit card to every file: the average gain was small on the Fed's model, but **46.8% of thin-file subprime borrowers moved from subprime to near-prime**. Thin files gain the most; files with recent delinquencies gain the least (only 7.9% of subprime borrowers with 2+ recent late payments moved up a tier). His file is damaged by fraud, not by his own late payments — so once the fraud accounts are blocked, the AU tradeline should work near its best case.
- AU accounts typically appear on the report within **30–60 days** of being added (his was added years ago — it is already there).
- FICO 8 and newer count AU accounts but filter suspected **purchased** tradelines. His is a legitimate family AU (parents added him), which FICO scores normally. Buying a stranger's tradeline is a different thing — see B7.

**What it does NOT do:**
- It does not create income, and lenders underwrite loans on income + own-credit, not on AU history alone.
- **Mortgage underwriting is the hardest wall:** Fannie Mae guidelines say AU tradelines generally cannot be considered for manually underwritten loans. A mortgage lender will look past it.
- Card issuers can see it is an AU account. Some discount it; most still count the score it produces.

**Fastest legitimate way to convert it into his own lines:**
1. **Block the fraud first** (FCRA letters) — the AU tradeline's lift is masked while fraudulent accounts sit on the file.
2. **Open 1 secured card now** ($200 deposit, Discover it Secured or Capital One Platinum Secured) — this creates his OWN primary tradeline, which is what lenders actually weight. The AU tradeline lifts the score; the secured card proves he can manage his own account.
3. **After 6–8 months of perfect payments + graduation**, apply for 1 unsecured card (e.g., Chase Freedom Rise is built for thin files; Capital One Quicksilver) — the AU history + own primary history together should support a $500–$1,500 starting limit.
4. **Do not** add more AU accounts from strangers, and do not pay anyone for tradelines. One legitimate family AU is the asset; rented ones get filtered and can be treated as misrepresentation.

## B5. Collateral he might actually have or could build cheaply

## B6. Legal income documentation — what counts, how long it must be seasoned

**The role of income — every $1 of documented income unlocks how much borrowing?**

**The honest catch:**
- **True 0% vs deferred interest:** the cards above are **true 0%** — interest does not accrue during the intro period; when it ends, interest applies only to the *remaining* balance at the regular APR. **Deferred-interest** cards (common at furniture/electronics stores) are different: if the balance is not paid in full by the end of the promo, interest is charged **retroactively on the entire original amount** at ~30% APR. Never take a deferred-interest offer without reading that clause.
- **What happens at the end:** any balance left starts accruing at 17–28% APR. A $2,000 balance at 25% APR costs ~$42/mo in interest alone. The 0% period is a window, not free money.
- **Balance-transfer fee:** 3–5% of the amount moved ($30–$50 per $1,000). A "0% transfer" still costs 3–5% upfront.
- **The 0% lane is real but it is a 12-month-out lane**, unlocked when his score crosses ~670. It is not a now move, and chasing it now (applying and being denied) costs inquiries.

## E3. Cash advances — exact cost (the most expensive money there is)

**Terms (2026, major banks):** cash-advance fee **3–5% of the amount ($10 minimum)** + cash-advance APR **~25–30%** (average ~29–30% at major banks; credit unions ~18%) + **NO grace period** — interest starts accruing **the day the cash leaves the ATM** and compounds daily + ATM operator fee (~$3–$5). | Hold period | Fee | Interest | Total cost | Total repaid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 days | $50.00 | ~$24.97 | **~$74.97** | $1,074.97 |
| 90 days | $50.00 | ~$76.74 | **~$126.74** | $1,126.74 |

# PART F — LEGAL FAST-TRACK TO PROVABLE INCOME

## The boundary (stated once, then we move on)

The owner asked for "tricks" to prove income, "I don't care, find workarounds." Here is the line, and it does not move:

**NO fake pay stubs. NO fake employer letters. NO fake tax returns. NO fake 1099s. NO lying on a FAFSA, loan application, grant application, or credit application. NO "renting" someone else's income, NO buying a fake employment verification.**

Why: a false statement on a loan or credit application to an FDIC-insured bank or the SBA is **bank fraud under 18 U.S.C. § 1014 — fine up to $1,000,000 and up to 30 years in federal prison, per count** (statute text verified via uscode.house.gov / federalreserve.gov). Lying on a FAFSA is **federal student-aid fraud under 20 U.S.C. § 1097** (fines and imprisonment). He just got out after 11.5 years. A fraud conviction would destroy everything he is building — the businesses, the credit rebuild, the Pell eligibility, and his freedom. No "trick" is worth that. The rest of this part is the legal fast-track, and it is genuinely fast — the documentation clock starts the day the first real dollar lands in the account, not before.

## F1. Income does NOT have to be a W-2 job — what counts, for which product

Self-employment counts. Gig work counts. Freelance counts. What matters is **documentation**, and different products want different documents:

| Document | What it proves | Which product wants it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tax return + Schedule C** (profit/loss from business) | Annual self-employment income, after expenses | SBA 7(a), bank-statement mortgages (as a cross-check), any conventional lender; also the FAFSA's prior-prior-year income |
| **1099-NEC** (issued by any client who paid him $600+ in a year) | Income from a specific payer | SBA, personal-loan underwriters, the IRS (it IS the income record) |
| **1099-K** (from PayPal, Stripe, Square, etc.) | Card/online payment volume | Underwriters as supporting proof; IRS reporting threshold is now $20,000 AND 200+ transactions (restored by the One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act) |
| **Bank statements** (business account, 3–12 months) | Regular deposits = revenue pattern | Fintech/CDFI personal + business loans; bank-statement mortgages (12–24 months); business credit underwriting |
| **CPA-prepared profit-and-loss statement** | Monthly revenue, expenses, net — certified by a licensed tax professional | Bank-statement lenders (can substitute for a lower expense factor — a CPA letter certifying a 25% expense factor instead of the standard 50% doubles qualifying income); SBA |
| **Invoices + receipts + contracts** | Who paid, for what, when, how much | Every underwriter as supporting proof; the paper trail that makes bank deposits *explainable* |
| **Pay stubs (W-2)** | Current employment income | Personal loans, cards (stated income), apartment rental, any lender that wants employment verification |
| **Award letters** (Social Security, disability, VA) | Government benefit income | Some lenders count these as qualifying income |

**The key insight:** a lender does not care whether the money came from a boss or a client. It cares whether the money is **real, regular, traceable, and documented**. A $2,000/mo GEO-audit business with invoices, a business bank account, and 1099s from clients is *better* income proof than a $2,000/mo part-time job with no pay stubs.

## F2. Bank-statement loans for self-employed borrowers — real names, real requirements

**Important precision:** the product called a "bank-statement loan" is a **non-QM mortgage** (home purchase/refinance), not a general-purpose personal loan. It matters because the owner needs to know what it does and does not do.

**Typical 2026 requirements (from multiple lender program guides, May–Oct 2026):**
- **12 or 24 consecutive months** of personal or business bank statements (a few programs offer 3-month options, and 1099-based programs use 12–24 months of 1099s instead)
- **2+ years of self-employment** (1-year options exist on select programs), 25%+ ownership of the business
- **Credit score 620–680 minimum** depending on lender (640 typical; better pricing at 700+)
- **10–20% down payment** on a primary residence (10% at 700+ FICO; 15–20% standard)
- **3–12 months of reserves** (liquid assets)
- **DTI 43–50%**
- **Expense factor: business deposits are cut by ~50%** before they count as income — $20,000/mo of business deposits becomes $10,000 of qualifying income. A CPA letter certifying a lower expense factor (e.g., 25%) lifts that to $15,000.
- Loan amounts commonly **to $3M**; rates run higher than conventional mortgages.
- Lenders in this space (program guides found in research): 1st Nationwide Mortgage, Austin Capital Mortgage, Save Financial / iLoanCA, and most non-QM wholesale lenders.

**What this means for him:** a bank-statement mortgage is a **24-months-out** door (needs 2 years of self-employment + 12–24 months of statements + a down payment + a house to buy). It is NOT a fast-cash product. But the *principle* transfers: **for personal loans, business loans, and CDFI loans, 3–6 months of clean business bank deposits are the single most persuasive document he can produce**, and the clock on those deposits starts this week.

**For non-mortgage borrowing, the "bank statement" equivalents are:**
- **CDFI / microloan intermediaries:** many accept 3 months of personal bank statements in place of business statements for pre-revenue startups (per SBA microloan guides).
- **Fintech personal loans (SoFi, Upstart, LendingClub, etc.):** underwrite on bank-statement cash flow + credit score; typically want 3–6 months of deposits and a score of 620+.
- **Revenue-based financing / merchant cash advance:** needs 6+ months of consistent business deposits — and carries very high effective costs (factor rates of 1.2–1.5x). **Use only as a last resort, with the full cost computed first.** An MCA is not a loan (it is a purchase of future receivables) and is lightly regulated — that is a warning, not a feature.

- **A part-time W-2 for 1–2 pay cycles as a base.** A part-time job (retail, warehouse, food service in Flagstaff) produces verifiable pay stubs in 2–4 weeks. It also satisfies the SNAP ABAWD 20-hr/week work requirement (see C2). This is the fastest single credibility boost available — and it is also just income.
- **Separate the business account NOW, before the first deposit.** A lender looking at 6 months of statements wants to see 6 months of *business* deposits in a *business* account. Six months of mixed personal/business deposits in a personal account is much weaker evidence. The clock starts when the account opens and the first business dollar lands.
- **Use a CPA or enrolled agent for the first Schedule C.** A CPA-prepared P&L is accepted by bank-statement lenders where a self-prepared one may not be. Cost: a few hundred dollars — worth it in year one, when the P&L is doing the most work.

## Part F bottom line

**The fastest legal route to provable income is to start generating and documenting real income THIS WEEK. The documentation clock starts the day the first dollar lands in the business account, not before.** There is no paperwork shortcut, no form, no "trick" that creates seasoned income — but there is also no waiting period before he can start: the first invoice, the first deposit, and the first 1099 can all happen this week, and in 90 days that paper trail is worth more than any loan product he could buy. ## G1. GoFundMe — how it works, fees, what makes it work, honest amounts

**Mechanics:** Create a fundraiser (free), write the story, add photos/video, set a goal (goals are not binding — you keep what is raised even if the goal is missed; there is no all-or-nothing rule on GoFundMe). Share the link. Donors give by card, PayPal, Venmo, Apple Pay, or Google Pay. Funds can be transferred to a verified bank account **at any time, even before reaching the goal**. Individual donations take up to 5 business days to process before being included in a transfer; transfers take 2–5 business days to reach the bank. Requires: a bank account in the organizer's/beneficiary's name + ID verification.

**Fees (verified, GoFundMe's own help center + pricing page, 2026):**
- **$0 platform fee** to start or manage a US personal fundraiser.
- **Transaction fee: 2.9% + $0.30 per donation** (2.2% + $0.30 for certified charity fundraisers — his campaign is a personal fundraiser, so 2.9% applies).
- GoFundMe's own example: a **$50 donation → $48.25** received. A $1,000 campaign from ten $100 gifts nets ~$968.
- Donors may leave an **optional tip** to GoFundMe (adjustable to $0) — it does not reduce his payout.
- **Recurring (monthly) donations:** the donor pays an extra **5% fee** per donation. That does not reduce his payout, but it is money the supporter spent that did not reach him.

**What makes a campaign work (GoFundMe's own guidance + observed patterns):**
- A **specific, truthful story** with a specific goal amount tied to specific costs ("$X for spine therapy sessions at $Y each" beats "help me get back on my feet").
- **Real photos and a video** of him telling the story in his own words. Campaigns with a personal video raise materially more than text-only pages (GoFundMe's own materials stress this; no verified multiplier figure was found in this session, so no number is claimed).
- **Early donations from people who know him** (family, friends) in the first 24–48 hours — campaigns that start with donations from their own circle are far more likely to be shared by strangers. Social proof is the engine.
- **Regular updates** (weekly at first): what the money paid for, what changed, what's next. Donors give again to campaigns that show the money working.
- **Sharing everywhere, repeatedly** — the link alone does nothing; it has to travel through his social channels, his content, and his network.

**Realistic amounts — honest ranges, not hype:** I could not verify a published median for personal GoFundMe campaigns in this session, so I will not invent one. The honest planning frame is:
- **Most personal campaigns raise hundreds to low thousands, from the organizer's own network**, unless the story spreads beyond it. A first push to family/friends/followers realistically raises **$500–$2,000**.
- **Viral upside exists but is not plannable:** his comeback story is genuinely compelling (11.5 years inside, rebuilding from zero, real businesses, real health need) and his chosen arena IS going viral as a real person. If the story catches — a video, a post, a share by someone with reach — $10,000–$50,000+ is possible. But it cannot be budgeted. Plan on $500–$2,000; work for the upside.
- **The goal amount matters psychologically:** a $5,000 goal that gets to 40% looks stalled; a $2,500 goal that hits 100% looks like a win and attracts more. Set the first goal at the **specific near-term need** (e.g., a defined round of therapies), not at a dream number. Raise the goal after it is met.

**Tax treatment:** personal-cause donations given out of generosity with nothing expected in return are **gifts, not taxable income** (26 U.S.C. § 102; IRS crowdfunding guidance). He owes no income tax on them, whether the campaign raises $500 or $500,000. The line flips if donors receive something of value in return (a product, a service, a share of profits) — then it is business income on Schedule C, plus 15.3% self-employment tax. **1099-K:** payment processors must file a 1099-K only when payments exceed **$20,000 AND 200+ transactions** in a calendar year (threshold restored by the One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act). A 1099-K arriving does not make the money taxable — the form reports volume, not taxability — but it must be dealt with on the return (report and exclude as gifts, with records). Keep every receipt for what the money was spent on.

**Rules:** the story must be truthful; funds must be used as stated; GoFundMe's Trust & Safety team verifies fundraisers and can freeze/withhold funds if the story or the use of funds is misrepresented. Misleading donors can also be fraud under state consumer-protection law. The Giving Guarantee protects donors, not organizers who mislead.
